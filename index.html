How this was built, and what to watch for
  • Source: the City of Melbourne 2019 on-street car parking sensor archive (42.7 million arrival/departure intervals). Each bay’s state was sampled every 15 minutes from 06:00 to 22:00, Melbourne local time. A bay is “vacant” if its sensor reported no vehicle at that instant, “occupied” if it reported one, and grey if no interval covered that instant (sensor not yet installed, offline, or removed).
  • Sensors that replaced one another in the same bay were merged by bay id. Intervals flagged as stuck sensors (single readings lasting days) were dropped.
  • The 2019 archive has no coordinates. Bays are placed on the map along their street block (matched by street name and the two cross streets to today’s road corridors), ordered by street marker number and pushed to the signed side of the street. Positions are therefore approximate within the block; the block itself is correct. 142 bays on blocks that no longer exist are counted in the city totals but not drawn.
  • “Typical” colours use the 2019 average for the selected day type (weekday, Saturday, or Sunday and Victorian public holidays) and hour.
  • Prices are estimates. The City’s 2018–19 budget set on-street rates at $7.00/h in the central city and $4.00/h (≤2h), $2.50/h (3h), $2.20/h (4h) outside it, phased in during the second half of 2018 (ABC News, 15 May 2018). Each bay’s rate here comes from its 2019 sign (meter or ticket) and whether its block is inside the Hoddle Grid. Loading zones, disabled bays and unmetered bays are counted as free. Actual meter rates could differ by block and time of day.
  • Restriction signs come from the most-seen sign for each sensor in 2019; time limits from the City’s current bay-restriction table where the sign was blank, so a few may differ from 2019.
